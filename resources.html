The four calculus instruments get their own drill — micro sickle, mesial curette, distal curette, buccal-lingual curette — because choosing the right tip for the tooth and surface is a recognition skill that simulation teaches well. Grip, stroke, feel and haptic precision are explicitly not taught.

Methods

Method 1

Four-handed dentistry

Clock-face zones (operator 7–12, transfer 4–7 below the chin, assistant 2–4, static 12–2); the single-handed transfer in four stages — working → signal → pretransfer → midtransfer; pen, palm and palm-thumb grasps; motion economy. The point: conserve motion, keep the operator's eyes on the illuminated field.

Source: dentalcare.com CE643; HDAS guide.

Method 2

The infection-control chain

Hand hygiene and PPE as Standard Precautions; the one-way reprocessing chain (Session 3); handpieces heat-sterilised between every patient; waterlines logged daily. Infection control is taught as a system, not a poster.

Source: CDC MMWR 2003;52(RR17), nine recommendation areas.

Method 3

Charting & records

FDI notation (with US Universal numbers shown alongside), the intake log, the sterilisation log, the retake log — one rule in every room: chart it, or it did not happen. The record is part of the care; it is also where the ADEA process of care ends — Evaluation & Documentation.

Sources: DANB GC documentation topics; ADEA process of care (DH.2).

Method 4

Radiography — positioning, never exposure

Paralleling vs bisecting-angle positioning, ALARA discipline, apron and thyroid collar — as a positioning drill only. The X-ray in the simulator is simulated: no real exposure is possible or claimed.

Source: DANB Radiation Health and Safety (RHS) topics.

Method 5

Prevention in the field

Sealant application in the order that makes it stick; fluoride varnish — the school-programme workhorse; and the screening workflow itself: screen → educate → refer, with consent checked first and comfort treated as a skill.

Sources: the Zero Cavity workflow (public); programme research notes.

Method 6

Communication, comfort & ergonomics

Patient communication and comfort (the comfort meter's rules), plus ergonomics and stress — the fourth hand is posture. The assistant's part in medical-emergency basics is drilled at Station F.

Sources: CE643 health-issues material; programme methods library.

Standards & pathways — the licensure bridge

The line that never moves: VR practice is not clinical hours. Simulation is not certification. This training makes no licensure claims and issues no credentials. Licensure is external only — a CODA-accredited program, the state board, DANB, NBDHE, state X-ray certification. What this page does is map the training straight to those real routes, so a learner can see what a credential actually requires.

RouteWhat it actually requiresSource
DANB Certified Dental Assistant (CDA) $450 exam fee · 195 minutes · 245 questions — RHS + ICE + GC taken together. Pathways: CODA-accredited program graduate, or high-school diploma + ≥3,500 hours approved work experience, or former CDA / dental degree routes — plus current hands-on CPR/BLS/ACLS. danb.org
California Registered Dental Assistant (RDA) Board-approved program ≥800 hours = 275 didactic + 260 lab/preclinical + 265 extramural clinical; or 15 months + ≥1,280 hours work experience; or current DANB CDA. Exam: RDA Combined Written and Law and Ethics. No reciprocity in California. Dental Board of California
Infection control guidance CDC Guidelines for Infection Control in Dental Health-Care Settings — 2003: nine recommendation areas, the spine of Sessions 2 and 3 and Station A/C. CDC MMWR 2003;52(RR17)
Competency frameworks CODA accreditation standards (dental assisting & dental hygiene) and the ADEA competencies / process of care — used here as design references for the station map, never as accreditation. CODA (ADA) · ADEA
Costa Rica No verified standalone assistant/hygienist licence is presented here. Learning paths. The library arranges the same material two ways: an assistant track (chairside, sterilisation, radiography) and a hygiene track, each mapped to the DANB CDA domains (RHS / ICE / GC) and the California RDA hour structure — with progress that is practice-only, never a credential. A glossary and printable checklists (screening morning, sterilisation chain, tray setup) round out the hub.
